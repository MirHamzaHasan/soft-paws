# BarkLog Design Theory

## Purpose

This document defines what BarkLog should feel like, why it should feel that way, and how to resolve the tension between a modern productivity UI and a warm caregiving journal.

## Product Thesis

BarkLog is not a generic pet app, not a clinic dashboard, and not a playful lifestyle tracker.

BarkLog should feel like:

- a calm caregiving instrument
- a personal health journal for one dog or a household of dogs
- a tool that becomes more valuable the more consistently it is used

The product wins when logging feels immediate, review feels trustworthy, and insight feels earned.

## Core Experience Model

BarkLog sits at the intersection of three modes:

### 1. Routine Mode

This is the dominant mode. The user is feeding, walking, observing, medicating, or cleaning up after a dog. They need zero-friction capture.

Design consequence:

- the first screen must make logging obvious without explanation
- routine actions should be one tap by default
- nuance belongs behind long-press, detail sheets, or follow-up screens

### 2. Reflection Mode

The user wants to understand trends, patterns, and context. They are not in a panic, but they want clarity.

Design consequence:

- the interface must shift from event capture to narrative interpretation
- charts support insight, but should not dominate the experience
- AI should summarize meaning, not overwhelm the user with dashboards

### 3. Urgency Mode

The user is worried. Their dog may have eaten something dangerous, is showing concerning symptoms, or needs immediate attention.

Design consequence:

- emergency surfaces must feel sober, direct, and calm
- action beats exploration
- trust beats beauty

## The Central Aesthetic Tension

The design spec says `Clean & modern — Notion/Linear vibe`, while the PRD says `The Tactile Health Journal` with `Parchment & Ink`.

These are not contradictory if interpreted correctly.

The correct synthesis is:

- modern information architecture
- paper-like restraint
- tactile clarity without skeuomorphism

This means BarkLog should not look like a literal leather journal, nor should it look like a glossy SaaS dashboard.

The target is:

- structurally modern
- visually quiet
- emotionally warm through relevance, not decoration

## Design Principles Interpreted

### Ink On Paper

This is the core metaphor.

It does not mean vintage textures, grain, or faux materials.
It means:

- high-contrast readable text
- warm light surfaces
- restrained components
- content-first composition

### One-Tap Obsessed

The best BarkLog screen is one where a dog parent does not need to think before acting.

This means:

- no tutorial dependence
- giant primary targets for repeated actions
- immediate confirmation
- progressive disclosure for detail

### Zero Decoration

BarkLog should never look empty because decoration was removed.
It should look intentional because hierarchy, rhythm, and proportion do the work.

This means:

- whitespace replaces flourish
- borders replace shadows
- typography replaces visual noise
- color exists for meaning, not excitement

### Dog-Only

This is not just taxonomy. It is product confidence.

The language, imagery, examples, and even AI context should be specifically dog-first.
The product should feel like it understands dog care instead of trying to be a general pet operating system.

## Emotional Targets

Every BarkLog screen should reinforce at least one of these emotional outcomes:

- `I can log this instantly.`
- `I understand what is happening.`
- `I know what to do next.`
- `This app remembers what matters.`

If a screen creates novelty but weakens those outcomes, it is off-strategy.

## Information Hierarchy Theory

The product should use three levels of emphasis:

### Primary

- the next action
- the most important question on the screen
- the dog's current status

### Secondary

- supporting data
- historical detail
- contextual qualifiers

### Tertiary

- metadata
- timestamps
- explanatory copy
- optional actions

The design should not flatten these levels. BarkLog works when the user always knows what matters most right now.

## Color Theory For BarkLog

The grayscale palette establishes trust.
The event palette establishes memory.
Semantic colors establish urgency and status.

Color should serve recognition:

- peach means poop
- blue means pee
- green means meal
- amber means walk
- purple means medication or AI
- red means symptom severity or danger

Color should never exist to make the interface feel more lively.

## Typography Theory

Inter 400 and 500 only is a deliberate constraint.

The product should feel:

- editorial but not luxurious
- digital but not corporate
- crisp but not sterile

The narrow weight range forces layout, spacing, and content to carry hierarchy instead of font theatrics.

## Premium Theory

The free product must remain useful enough to become trusted.
Premium should unlock leverage, depth, and professional output.

Free should answer:

- what happened today
- what did I log
- what do I need to remember

Premium should answer:

- what pattern is emerging
- what should I bring to the vet
- how do I manage multiple dogs or richer records

This protects both trust and conversion.

## BarkLog Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- over-gamified pet-care UI
- cheerful emergency design
- dashboard clutter that slows routine logging
- decorative gradients and soft-shadow cards
- medical certainty in AI or triage language
- paywalls that interrupt urgent or basic care tasks

## Design Review Questions

Before approving any BarkLog design, ask:

1. Is the primary action obvious in under two seconds?
2. Does this screen look trustworthy without looking clinical?
3. Is color carrying meaning or merely adding energy?
4. Can a stressed dog owner use this without extra explanation?
5. Does the screen feel like BarkLog, not a generic mobile app?
